# A public earnings estimate is not a creator's payment record

A creator-research sheet can contain a public earnings range without containing any evidence of money paid to that creator. Keep those two claims separate before forwarding a shortlist, writing a profile or briefing a client.

This is a source-labeling checklist, not an earnings calculator, accounting advice or a promise of monetization. TubeText Labs maintains the optional tools linked below. Prepared with AI assistance from documentation; no Actor, payment account or customer result was tested for this guide.

## Start with the question, not a scraper

For your own channel, use your existing authorized YouTube Studio and AdSense for YouTube access. You do not need a paid public-data tool to inspect your own available reports. For another creator, use only what they have deliberately shared and label its scope; a public handle does not grant access to their private financial reports. Do not request passwords, access tokens or unredacted payment screenshots.

YouTube distinguishes estimated revenue in Analytics from finalized earnings in AdSense for YouTube; the amounts can differ after adjustments. Its current instructions explain where the owner can inspect each. [Check your YouTube revenue](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314488?hl=en).

Finalization, payment issuance and receipt are also different events. YouTube describes payment conditions and transfer times that depend on the payment method. An amount shown in a report is not, by itself, evidence that a bank has received it. Check your own relevant payment status and receipt record rather than inferring arrival from public views. [YouTube payment process](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14728151?hl=en).

## Keep four evidence categories separate

These are analyst labels, not new Actor output fields:

| Category | What to record | What not to claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public third-party estimate | Provider, creator identifier, original range, currency, period label and observation time | Actual revenue, a guaranteed minimum, or a payment |
| Owner's platform estimate | Authorized report, selected period, metric and visible adjustment status | A finalized amount or money received |
| Finalized platform earnings | Authorized account record, applicable period and amount | Automatic proof that a transfer arrived |
| Payment or receipt evidence | The specific event, date, currency and source the owner has checked | Total business profit or complete income from every activity |

Keep the event name explicit: an AdSense Payment Receipt records a payment from Google; it does not by itself establish that the money is available in the recipient's account. Google's [wire-transfer FAQ](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/6025222?hl=en) distinguishes a transfer reaching the bank from the bank applying it to the account. Do not relabel one event as the other.

Do not fill a missing category with a value copied from another. A public range does not verify the owner's sponsorship fees, expenses or net profit. Nor should it be described as a statistical confidence interval unless its source actually establishes that meaning.

## A small, invented research note

Suppose a fictional creator's public record shows a monthly estimate of **USD 100–1,000**. A defensible note is:

> Third-party monthly earnings estimate: USD 100–1,000; source and observation date retained. Owner-authorized revenue and payment records not reviewed. Actual earnings and amounts received unknown.

Do not replace that range with its midpoint, call the lower endpoint a minimum paycheck, or describe the creator as earning USD 1,000. This invented example illustrates labeling only; it is not a real creator, a product output or a calculation method.

If the estimate is absent, write **unavailable in the observed source**, not zero. Preserve the original period label: a monthly estimate is not necessarily an observed calendar month's receipts. If you compare two observations, their difference is a change in displayed estimates—not verified growth in paid income.
